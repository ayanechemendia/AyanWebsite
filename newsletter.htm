Technology Notes: Learning by Building

Fall 2026 Edition   |   By Ayan Felipe Echemendia-Carranza

Welcome to my technology newsletter, a place to share what I am learning as I continue studying computer programming. Technology is part of everyday life, but creating software takes patience, planning, and practice. Through my college classes, I am learning to break problems into smaller steps, test possible solutions, and improve my work when something does not behave as expected.

Building Programming Skills

One of the most useful lessons in programming is that mistakes are part of the learning process. A compiler message or a webpage that does not look right can point toward something that needs attention. Reading the message carefully, checking the code, and testing one change at a time makes it easier to find the cause. As I continue practicing Java, Python, HTML, and CSS, I want to become more confident at solving problems and explaining how my solutions work.
SMALL STEPS, CONSISTENT PRACTICE, AND CURIOSITY HELP TURN IDEAS INTO PROJECTS.

What I Am Exploring

Web development combines structure, design, and accessibility. HTML organizes the information on a page, while CSS controls the layout, colors, spacing, and responsive behavior. I have also been learning how navigation links, images, and shared stylesheets help a multi-page website feel consistent. Reviewing a page on both a computer and a phone is important because visitors should be able to read and use the site on different screen sizes.

My Current Learning Checklist

These are some of the skills I am continuing to develop through coursework and practice:

  • Write and test Java and Python programs.
  • Build clear HTML pages and organize content logically.
  • Use CSS to create consistent, responsive page layouts.
  • Debug errors patiently and document improvements.

Looking Ahead

My goal is to complete my Computer Programmer Certificate while continuing to improve my technical and professional skills. Each assignment gives me a chance to practice accuracy, persistence, and problem solving. I hope to keep building projects that show what I have learned and help me prepare for future opportunities in the technology field. Thank you for reading this edition of my newsletter.
